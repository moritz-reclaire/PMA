3 · Culture, Communication & Leadership

Lecture 3 (Möller, 07/05/2026) with a guest lecture by Dr. Tilo Nemuth and Lukas Conzen (Midstad Development GmbH) on innovative strategies for redeveloping urban properties across Europe. The core claim of the lecture: "Technical expertise alone is not enough in international projects."

Why this is measurable (The Economist Intelligence Unit 2012, Competing Across Borders): in international (virtual) project teams
  • it takes over 50 % longer to vote and make decisions
  • 20–40 % more person-hours are needed for the communication effort
The biggest obstacles to successful collaboration: misunderstandings due to different cultural traditions, and various standards in the workplace.

What is culture?

The lecture opens with a picture: pierogi, ravioli, Maultaschen, jiǎozi, gyōza, momos, samosas, empanadas, pelmeni … — "See the same thing but mean something different, or talk about the same thing but see different things." Followed by the Tower of Babel (Genesis 11) as the archetype of a language confusion that brings a construction project to a halt.

Three definitions:
  • Hofstede: "Culture is the collective programming of the mind that distinguishes members of one group or category of people from another."
  • Trompenaars: "Culture is the way human societies find solutions to problems."
  • A. Thomas: "Culture is a universal, but for a nation, society, organization and group very specific orientation system. It influences the perception, thinking, values and actions of all members and thus defines their membership in the society, organization and group."

Ethnocentrism

Ethnocentrism is the tendency to believe that one's own cultural views and methods are best and should be used as the standard (after Hesse/Schrader, Intercultural Competency Training).

Two examples from the lecture: "I believe there is no alternative to this approach." (German chancellor Angela Merkel, 2009, 2010) and "I know much more than he does about interest rates, believe me." (US President Donald Trump about Jerome Powell, chair of the Federal Reserve, 29 April 2025).

Intercultural competence development according to M. J. Bennett

Six stages — the first three ethnocentric, the last three ethnorelative; the transition is intercultural learning:
  1. Denial — there is only one's own way
  2. Defence — your own way is the best way
  3. Downplay — the differences are irrelevant
  4. Accept — there are different ways to do/see things
  5. Adapt — use new methods to act more effectively
  6. Integrate — smooth transition between different cultures

  • great mental flexibility
  • language skills — very good to contract proof
  • tolerance towards other cultures
  • ability to work in a team
  • loyalty and ability to "balance" conflicting interests
  • intercultural skills
  • assertive
  • decision-making competence
  • generalist with high technical general competence in project management or specialist in technical construction management
  • contract reliability
  • head office knowledge and contacts

Factors influencing the success of the project

Team building: the Tuckman phase model

Possible actions of the project manager in the intercultural team building process: kick-off event · information about the new culture · organization of an intercultural training · facilitate exchange · allocation of tasks according to strengths · driving the solution finding process · common goals.

Development of an intercultural project team

Project execution in intercultural teams — six guiding questions

  • Documentation — in what form and how are the results documented? How often is something recorded in writing?
  • Quality management — what importance is placed on troubleshooting? Is the culture action-oriented or results-oriented?
  • Risk management — how high is the need for uncertainty avoidance?
  • Information management — how high is the importance of informal information flow? What is the role of the project manager in the hierarchy?
  • Scheduling — how detailed is the scheduling? How strictly is the plan adhered to?
  • Contact maintenance — is the culture relationship-oriented? How much time is invested in building and maintaining good relationships?

Success factors for intercultural cooperation

Good project organisation (tasks and responsibilities, processes) · willingness to accept constructive criticism · positive basic attitude · cultural awareness · solid language skills.

Important for all team members: a mixture of hard and soft skills.
