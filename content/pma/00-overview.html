Overview & Exam Guide

Projectmanagement Advanced (Summer Semester 2026, Dr. oec. Dipl.-Ing. oec. Olaf Möller, External Lecturer STRABAG · Michelle Kaus M.Sc., ICoM RWTH Aachen). The course covers international construction project management — from organizational structures and strategies through financing, culture and contract law to risk and conflict management. This collection summarises all seven lectures including the guest lectures.

Examination format: DYNEXITE exam on 06/08/2026, 9–10 am (60 minutes). Plus a term paper as group work (case study, 5–6 people, 30 pages + attachment, plus a 10-minute PPT video pitch), submission 13/08/2026 11:59 pm via RWTHmoodle.

The guest lectures are explicitly exam-relevant. That is stated in the semester schedule — they are not optional extras.

Course structure

Six thematic blocks, each a 3-hour lecture with a guest lecture (Thursdays 2:15–5:15 pm, PPS H2), plus five one-hour exercises with consultation time.

How this collection is organised

  • 1 · Basics, Organization & Strategies — project definitions (DIN 69901, PMI, PRINCE2, ISO 21500), international construction projects and their case studies, expatriates, staffing approaches, organizational forms, fields of activity, stakeholders, the FIDIC base structure.
  • 2 · Financing, Procurement & Logistics — project development and financing models, guarantees and Hermes cover, financiers, strategic procurement, sourcing, logistics, transport systems and Incoterms. Plus the export finance guest lecture (ECA/PRI).
  • 3 · Culture, Communication & Leadership — the concept of culture, iceberg model, Osgood/Schein, Lewis culture types, Hofstede dimensions, ethnocentrism, Bennett's model, Tuckman, GLOBE leadership styles, communication media.
  • 4 · International Contract Law — forms of remuneration, standard contract forms, FIDIC (Rainbow Suite, Golden Principles, General Conditions), contract documents, the role of the Engineer, claims and the dispute escalation ladder.
  • 5 · Innovation, Digitalisation & Sustainability — megatrends, BIM and the digital construction site, life cycle cost, circular economy (9R), CO₂e accounting (CCF/PCF), the climate neutrality pathway. Plus the guest lecture on water, dams and pumped storage hydro.
  • 6 · Risk & Conflict Management — risk definition and risk types, the risk management process per ISO 31000, risk map, methods, treatment strategies, estimating. Plus the Technical Controlling guest lecture.
  • 7 · Recap & Career — the "You should know" lists of every lecture in one place, plus career paths and practical tips.

Exam relevance — the "You should know" lists

Every lecture ends with a Lessons Learned slide. These lists are the most reliable exam guidance in the whole course; Lecture 7 repeats all of them together. In this collection they appear at the end of each chapter page.

Two pieces of advice the lecturer repeats several times:
  • "English is the lingua franca. Learn, understand and use the right technical terms!" — the English technical vocabulary (FIDIC terminology, Incoterms, ECA …) is the actual subject matter.
  • "Studying literature cannot replace practice, especially in the international construction business!" — flanked by the Gorky quote "I did not graduate from any university, except the university of life."

Recommended literature

  • Kulick, R.: Auslandsbau (2010) — by far the most frequently cited source in the lecture. If you read only one book, read this one.
  • Liebchen et al.: Baumanagement und Bauökonomie (2007)
  • Micksch: Bauleitung im Ausland (2016)
  • Bauer: Baubetrieb (2007)
  • Hauptverband der Deutschen Bauindustrie e.V. — Internationales Bauen
  • Journals: International Journal of Construction Management, Engineering, Construction and Architectural Management
