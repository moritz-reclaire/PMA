Project development and financing

The project development process starts from one of three constellations: location seeks capital and project idea — project seeks location and capital — capital seeks location and project idea. From there:

  1. rough description of the main features of the project (use, areas)
  2. simple project development calculation (frontdoor/backdoor approach)
  3. project concept
  4. feasibility analysis — market analysis, location analysis, analysis of the utilization concept, competition analysis, risk analysis; plus profitability and yield analysis
  5. project concretization — realization decision, conclusion of contract, project partner declaration, approval planning
  6. project management — commercial project management and technical project management (deadlines, costs, quality)

Running in parallel: project/property securing, project marketing, investment, renting, sale.

Investors

Three groups: private investors (self-use, capital investment, direct and indirect investment) · institutional investors — single asset portfolio (open-end and closed-end real estate funds, real estate AG holding, mixed forms) and multi asset portfolio (insurance companies, pension funds, leasing companies, foreign investors) · company (self-use, production, store management, sell, research, capital investment).

Insurances & guarantees

Guarantee in national construction projects

A three-party relationship: the bank (guarantor) provides a guarantee for the debtor — secondary liability. The construction company (contractor, debtor) owes the performance of the contract — primary liability. The builder (client, creditor) owes the remuneration. Between bank and contractor sits the guarantee contract.

Guarantees in traditional foreign construction

The contractor provides the client with security against defective performance of the contract — four types of guarantee along the project phases:
  1. Tender Guarantee (Bid Bond)
  2. Advance Payment Guarantee
  3. Performance Guarantee
  4. Warranty Obligations Guarantee
Foreign construction adds two further relationships:
  • Customs Guarantee towards the customs authorities in the host country (customs obligations)
  • Hermes cover from the Federal Republic of Germany against economic and political risks — covers the construction company's losses

Guest lecture: export finance (exficon)

Export in foreign trade refers to the cross-border sale of goods or services from the domestic market to foreign countries. The starting situation: there is great need for investments or procurements — but from the exporter's perspective, the necessary financing must be secured.

Four stages of project development

ECA — Export Credit Agency

Definition: An export credit agency or credit insurance agency is a private or government-related institution that acts as an intermediary between national governments and exporters to provide export insurance covers and financial guarantees. ECAs are considered (state-backed) instruments for export promotion. They are commercial support tools and therefore applicable exclusively to commercial financings.

In Germany, Euler Hermes Kreditversicherungs-AG is entrusted with implementing the guarantee instruments as the Federal Government's mandatary. Three types of guarantee: export credit guarantees (the "Hermes covers"), investment guarantees and guarantees for untied loans (UFK / Ungebundene Finanzkredite).

Reasons for involving an ECA:

  • the borrower's creditworthiness is not sufficient to grant a loan (possibly in the requested amount) without collateral
  • the requested loan tenor is commercially not feasible
  • there is a long lead time for the delivery / service — banks generally shy away from long waiting times for the first repayment
  • an ECA-covered tranche is eligible as a cover pool for banks, i.e. refinancing via the capital market becomes possible

Advantages: more attractive conditions than purely commercial financing (more favorable and longer terms), with the option to combine with other foreign trade promotion instruments (e.g. the fixed interest rate programme CIRR, funding for feasibility studies). Depending on project size, financing may only be possible with (partial) coverage — especially in more "exotic" countries.

Application process (identical across the OECD)

  1. Submission of application — the exporter and, if applicable, the bank submit the application for cover
  2. Review process — the ECA reviews and decides if the transaction is < €5m and no special restrictions apply; environmental and social assessment may be required
  3. Review by decision committee — applications > €10m must be submitted to the IMA (interministerial committee); a unanimous decision is required
  4. Cover decision — preliminary cover commitment letter to be issued, possibly approval with conditions or collateral requirements, additional information required, or rejection
  5. Notification post signing — the exporter and bank notify the ECA of the contract signing, receipt of securities, and that requirements have been fulfilled
  6. Notification of final cover — the ECA issues the cover document and the premium invoice
  7. Payment of the premium — the importer pays the premium (alternatively from the ECA-covered loan), thereby activating the cover (previously: pending ineffectiveness)

Types of coverage and protected risks

The following basic principles apply:
  • coverage is available at all times against insolvency of the buyer, governmental or legislative interventions, and war/revolution/uprising (political risks)
  • a mandatory retainer of 5–15 % applies for economic risks and 5 % for political risks
  • the application for coverage must be submitted strictly before the start of the risk
Excluded countries (as per the lecture): Sierra Leone, Somalia, North Korea, Syria, Sudan, Congo (DRC).

Structures

  • Buyer credit — the bank finances the buyer directly; special feature: buyer and borrower are not identical
  • Supplier credit — the exporter as indirect financier
  • Onlending structure — if the creditworthiness of the buyer is insufficient

OECD Consensus

Applicable to export projects with a tenor > 2 years.

New since 04/2023 — classification of all cover applications into three categories:
  1. Green category — particularly eligible (green) technologies, e.g. bioenergy, green hydrogen, district heating & cooling networks → simplified and more attractive cover conditions
  2. White category — no significant contribution to the Paris climate goals, but also not in contradiction to the 1.5-degree target → conditions unchanged (also applies to decommissioning of fossil energy infrastructure / conversion to non-fossil)
  3. Red category — not compatible with the 1.5-degree target, e.g. coal-fired power plants or oil extraction → exclusion from cover
Basic conditions: loan amount max. 85 % of the contract value (15 % advance and interim payment) · local costs since 07/2023 up to 70 % of the contract value (green category) · at least one payment per year (in connection with interest payment) · starting point depending on delivery, at the latest operational readiness (if installation is the responsibility of the exporter) · loan term max. 18 years, green category up to 22 years · premium discount for long loan terms (green category only).

Sustainable lending

Anchored in the OECD Principles and Guidelines to Promote Sustainable Lending Practices in the Provision of Official Export Credits to Lower Income Countries (11/2016). HIPC and MDRI countries have been relieved of debt, but structural problems still often persist. To prevent foreseeable renewed over-indebtedness, the OECD countries and their ECAs have committed to limiting commercial borrowing (and accordingly restricting cover practices) as soon as the countries receive IMF or World Bank support under ongoing programmes. In such cases development loans should include a grant element of up to 35 %; for commercial loans the country receives a Commercial Lending Window, based on progress in national budget consolidation and subject to annual review. ECA-covered financings in the application process for these countries must be reported to the IMF / World Bank — without approval, the cover may not be legally granted.

HIPC Heavily Indebted Poor Countries Initiative for Debt Relief · MDRI Multilateral Debt Relief Initiative · DSSI Debt Service Suspension Initiative (World Bank and G20).

Strategic procurement

Starting points and goals of strategic procurement, with six levers around it: volume concentration, product improvement, process improvement, customer relationship, global procurement, market/price evaluations.

The procurement process in 8 steps

  1. Develop general product and supplier information — gather usage data, develop a technical overview, compile procurement opportunities, compare conditions of sale
  2. Meet demands — assess current and future requirements, investigate substitution possibilities, business plan
  3. Assess supply market — procurement market analysis, identify potential suppliers, create a long list of qualified suppliers
  4. Set procurement strategy — select the strategy, develop an action plan, finalize procurement planning
  5. Select suppliers — tender the service/product, rate tenders, create a short list of providers, prepare negotiations
  6. Conduct negotiations — formulate goal and plan, conduct negotiations, justify the selection, conclude a contract
  7. Contract
  8. Integrate suppliers — and continuous evaluation
Steps 1–4 are strategy, steps 5–8 are tactic.

Sourcing strategies

Procurement financing

Central vs. decentralized procurement

Advantages of central procurement: higher demand and negotiating power towards suppliers = more favorable prices · uniform appearance → reduction of market segmentation efforts by suppliers · better enforceability of strategic procurement decisions (e.g. framework agreements) · less coordination effort · development of special knowledge about building materials and materials of complex trades · lower probability of bribery.

Disadvantages: bureaucratization tendencies → cumbersome and reduced flexibility · remote from operation and market → low employee satisfaction and motivation · does not know regional specifics.

Middle ground: the lead-buyer system.

Logistics

Logistics in the construction process accompanies the five phases planning building usage → building design → planning construction → construction → building usage; construction site logistics begins in phase 3/4, planning logistics before that.

Two kinds of logistic goods:
  • Personnel logistics — expatriates, TCNs and locals to the construction site
  • Material and equipment logistics — material and equipment from Germany, from third countries and from the local market

Logistics process chain

Material flow and information flow run in opposite directions: the construction site determines demand qualitatively and quantitatively → the building consultant / engineer approves the material (approval procedure) → the purchaser of the construction company orders the material → the supplier supplies it → the freight forwarder organizes transport and prepares the material → the transporter transports it → the construction site receives and stores it. Material not subject to an approval procedure (e.g. construction site equipment) skips the approval step.

Transport systems

A practical example of real durations: return of personal effects from Saudi Arabia to Germany (Riyadh – Jeddah – Rotterdam – Hamburg – Essen): 31.03. departure from construction site, 05.05. ship departs from Jeddah, 17.06. arrival notice Hamburg, 22.08. home delivery.

Spare parts logistics

Option 1 — provision of an extensive spare parts warehouse. Advantage: constant availability of many spare parts. Disadvantage: commitment of a large amount of capital for a long time.
Option 2 — quick purchase and transportation after demand determination. Advantage: exclusively purchasing required spare parts. Disadvantage: high purchase/transport costs per part.

Optimal: combining both methods — first phase option 1, second phase option 2. Outsourcing variant: consignment stock — goods owned by a supplier but stored at the premises of the customer.

For scale, the dam construction site Xiaolangdi, China: new value of earthmoving equipment approximately US$60 million, monthly spare parts consumption US$500,000–800,000, monthly fuel consumption approximately 2,500,000 litres, monthly tire consumption US$150,000–200,000 — of a total of ~1,550 people, approximately one third worked in the mechanical engineering department.

Incoterms

International Commercial Terms — trade clauses for contracting in international trade in goods. They define the responsibilities of buyers and sellers in the international trading system and allocate costs and risks. They are voluntarily integrable into a contract; the rulebook distinguishes 11 trading conditions.
Cost structure — how the price grows with each clause:
EXW price = purchase price ex work
+ loading in work
+ transport to quay in port of shipment
+ export costs  = FAS price
+ loading on board  = FOB price
+ sea freight to port of destination  = CFR price
+ marine insurance  = CIF price
+ unloading in port of destination  = DEQ price
+ import costs + transport from quay to construction site  = DDP price
